# Manuscript plan: EAAI special issue "IoT in Disaster Management" (VSI: EAAI_IoT Disaster Mgmt)

## Target topics covered
2 (Digital Twin for flood rise and gradient), 4 (AI/ML in IoT), 7 (ML for early warning), 9 (IoT in early warning), 8 (IoT surveillance/monitoring, via sensor-fault monitoring). Secondary: 5 (big-data analytics: ~100 gauges x 10 years x 15 min, about 47 M readings).

## Working title
**A Fault-Tolerant, Physics-Guided Digital Twin for Flood Stage-Rise and Gradient Early Warning from IoT River-Gauge Networks, with Conformal Uncertainty and Cost-Aware Alerting**

## Novelty claims (each backed by an experiment)
1. **Gradient-first forecasting**: the twin predicts the *rise rate* (ft/h) and *time-to-threshold*, not only stage. Warning is raised on predicted crossing with calibrated lead time.
2. **Topology-free physics prior**: upstream-downstream coupling and travel times are estimated from telemetry alone (lagged cross-correlation, Muskingum-style celerity). They are used as (a) a routing baseline and (b) a physics-guided feature/residual layer. No GIS river network is needed, so it deploys in data-poor basins.
3. **Fault-tolerant IoT layer**: a sensor-health module (spike, flatline, dropout, drift detection) plus a masked-input twin trained with stochastic sensor dropout. Evaluated under injected fault regimes (dropout 0-60 %, latency, drift, stuck-at).
4. **Calibrated early warning**: split-conformal and adaptive conformal intervals on stage and rise-rate; asymmetric (upper-risk) conformal bound for alerting; coverage under distribution shift.
5. **Decision-centric evaluation**: POD/FAR/CSI, lead-time gain, Peirce skill, cost-loss value curves, and an edge-compute budget (latency, memory, energy proxy) for gateway deployment.
6. **Cross-region generalisation**: leave-one-region-out over four hydro-climatically distinct regions (Mid-Atlantic, Texas Hill Country, Ohio Valley, Upper Midwest).
